## Overview

Fonema is an audio installation about artificial intelligence and human communication. Recorded voices pass through an artificial neural network. What comes back still sounds like speech in grain and rhythm, but sentences do not hold: enough to suggest a person, not enough to settle on a meaning.

It uses voice-classification and recognition-style models. The output is tuned to break lexical meaning, not to label speakers or transcribe speech.

## How it works

The work looks at neural architectures and audio signal-processing chains: how latent spaces and decision boundaries behave when you stop asking the model to label cleanly and keep it between word and noise. The voices can feel like overhearing a conversation you are not following.

In the installation, the focus is on how machine-mediated audio shapes whether spoken language feels trustworthy.

## Context

Fonema was a thesis project. Research, prototyping, and the installation form came out of that academic frame, not a commission.
